1. INTRODUCTION TO CONJUNCTIONS

Definition:

Conjunctions are words that join words, phrases, clauses, or sentences to make expressions smoother and more meaningful.

Purpose:

They help in showing relationships such as addition, contrast, cause-effect, time, condition, choice, etc.

Importance:

Without conjunctions, sentences would be short, choppy, and disconnected.

Examples:

- I like tea and coffee.

- She stayed at home because it was raining.

- You must work hard, or you will fail.

2. CLASSIFICATION OF CONJUNCTIONS

Conjunctions are mainly of three kinds:

A. Coordinating Conjunctions

Join two words, phrases, or independent clauses of equal rank.

List: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so (remembered by the acronym FANBOYS)

B. Subordinating Conjunctions

Join a dependent clause (subordinate clause) to an independent clause.

Common subordinating conjunctions: because, although, though, since, unless, until, if, when, while, before, after, as, as soon as, even though, in order that, so that, whereas, provided that, etc.

C. Correlative Conjunctions

Used in pairs to join parallel structures.

List: either…or, neither…nor, both…and, not only…but also, whether…or, such…that, so…that, as…as, rather…than, hardly…when, scarcely…when

D. Conjunctive Adverbs

however, therefore, moreover, nevertheless, otherwise, consequently, meanwhile

3. RULES WITH EXAMPLES FOR EACH CONJUNCTION

A. Coordinating Conjunctions (FANBOYS)

For – shows reason

- I stayed home, for I was not feeling well.

- He is respected, for he is honest.

- We cannot play, for it is raining.

And – shows addition

- She bought apples and oranges.

- He studies hard and plays football well.

- The teacher was kind and helpful.

Nor – shows negative addition

- He does not smoke, nor does he drink.

- She never smokes, nor does she drink.

- They had no money, nor food.

But – shows contrast

- I like mangoes, but I dislike papayas.

- She tried, but failed.

- I like tea, but not coffee.

Or – shows choice/alternative

- Do you want tea or coffee?

- You must study hard or you will fail.

- Call me tomorrow or send an email.

Yet – shows contrast, similar to "but"

- He is poor, yet honest.

- She is young, yet very wise.

- It is raining, yet they are playing outside.

So – shows result/consequence

- It rained heavily, so the match was cancelled.

- I studied well, so I passed.

- He was late, so the bus left without him.

B. Subordinating Conjunctions

Because – shows cause/reason

- I did not go out because it was raining.

- She cried because she lost her toy.

- He came late because there was traffic.

Although / Though – shows contrast

- She smiled although she was sad.

- Though she is rich, she is not proud.

- Although it rained, they played football.

Since – shows time or reason

- I have lived here since I was ten years old. (time)

- He left early since he was not well. (reason)

Unless – shows negative condition

- You cannot succeed unless you work hard.

- He cannot pass unless he studies.

Until / Till – shows time limit

- Wait here until I return.

- I will wait here till he comes.

If – shows condition

- You will pass if you study hard.

- If it rains, we will stay home.

When – shows time

- She was sleeping when I called her.

- I will call you when I reach home.

While – shows time or contrast

- Stay here while I make a call. (time)

- He works hard, while his brother wastes time. (contrast)

Before – shows time

- Finish your homework before you go to play.

After – shows time

- Wash your hands after you eat.

As – shows cause, manner, or time

- She failed as she did not prepare well. (cause)

- Do it as I told you. (manner)

- I looked up as he entered. (time)

Even though – strong contrast

- He continued working even though he was tired.

- She smiled even though she was sad.

In order that / So that – shows purpose

- I speak slowly so that you may understand.

- He left early in order that he could catch the bus.

Whereas – shows contrast

- I like coffee, whereas my brother likes tea.

Provided that – shows condition

- You may come, provided that you behave well.

C. Correlative Conjunctions

Either…or – shows choice

- You may take either tea or coffee.

- He will come either today or tomorrow.

Neither…nor – shows negative choice

- He is neither rich nor famous.

- She likes neither tea nor coffee.

Both…and – shows addition

- She is both intelligent and hardworking.

- He can sing both English and Nepali songs.

Not only…but also – shows emphasis/addition

- He is not only a singer but also a composer.

- She not only danced but also sang.

Whether…or – shows choice/doubt

- I don't know whether she will come or not.

- Tell me whether you like it or not.

Such…that – shows result

- It was such a cold day that we stayed indoors.

So…that – shows result

- He was so tired that he fell asleep at once.

As…as – shows comparison

- She is as tall as her brother.

- He is not as clever as his brother.

Rather…than – shows preference

- I would eat fruit rather than fast food.

- I prefer to stay home rather than go out.

Hardly…when / Scarcely…when – shows immediate succession

- I had hardly reached the station when the train arrived.

- She had scarcely entered the room when the phone rang.

4. COMPARATIVE STUDY OF CONJUNCTIONS

Table 1: Types of Conjunctions and Their Functions

Type of Conjunction Function Example Special Note
Coordinating Connects ideas of equal importance I like tea and coffee. Limited in number (FANBOYS).
Subordinating Connects dependent clause to main clause She stayed home because it rained. Creates complex sentences.
Correlative Works in pairs to join balanced ideas He is not only rich but also generous. Adds emphasis, balance, or choice.

Table 2: Comparative Study of Specific Conjunctions

Conjunctions Usage Example
because vs. although reason vs. contrast I stayed home because I was sick. / I went out although I was sick.
because of vs. in spite of / despite reason vs. contrast He failed because of his laziness. / He succeeded in spite of difficulties.
so vs. therefore both show result, but "therefore" is more formal I was tired, so I went to bed. / It rained; therefore, the match was cancelled.
while vs. whereas both show contrast; "while" also shows time She likes tea, whereas I like coffee. / He was singing while she was dancing.
despite vs. although "despite" + noun/gerund; "although" + clause He came despite the rain. / He came although it was raining.
however vs. therefore contrast vs. result She studied hard. However, she failed. / She studied hard; therefore, she passed.

5. COMMON RULES FOR USING CONJUNCTIONS

1. Do not use double conjunctions

❌ Although he is poor, but he is honest.

✅ Although he is poor, he is honest.

✅ He is poor, but he is honest.

2. Parallelism with correlative conjunctions

❌ He is either going to play or cinema.

✅ He is going either to the playground or to the cinema.

3. Use commas with coordinating conjunctions joining independent clauses

✅ She was tired, but she completed her work.

✅ It was raining, so we stayed home.

4. Choose the conjunction carefully to avoid changing meaning

✅ I came early because I wanted to help. (cause)

✅ I came early so that I could help. (purpose)

5. Position of conjunctions

- Coordinating conjunctions usually come between the words or clauses they join.

- Subordinating conjunctions can come at the beginning or middle of a sentence.

✅ Because it was raining, we stayed home.

✅ We stayed home because it was raining.

E. Transformation (20 items)

1. He did not come because he was ill. → He was ill, ___ he did not come. (so)

→ He was ill, so he did not come.

2. Although he is rich, he is unhappy. → He is rich, ___ he is unhappy. (but)

→ He is rich, but he is unhappy.

3. He could not attend school because of his illness. → He was ill, ___ he could not attend school. (so)

→ He was ill, so he could not attend school.

4. Despite his efforts, he failed. → He failed ___ he tried hard. (although)

→ He failed although he tried hard.

5. She is poor but honest. → She is honest, ___ she is poor. (although)

→ She is honest, although she is poor.

6. He will pass if he studies. → He will not pass, ___ he studies. (unless)

→ He will not pass, unless he studies.

7. She was tired, but she worked. → She worked ___ being tired. (in spite of)

→ She worked in spite of being tired.

8. He is both intelligent and hardworking. → He is not ___ intelligent ___ hardworking. (only...but also)

→ He is not only intelligent but also hardworking.

9. I like tea or coffee. → I like ___ tea ___ coffee. (either...or)

→ I like either tea or coffee.

10. He doesn't like tea. He doesn't like coffee. → He likes ___ tea ___ coffee. (neither...nor)

→ He likes neither tea nor coffee.

11. She was so tired that she couldn't walk. → She was ___ tired ___ she couldn't walk. (too...to)

→ She was too tired to walk.

12. The movie was very interesting. I watched it twice. → The movie was ___ interesting ___ I watched it twice. (so...that)

→ The movie was so interesting that I watched it twice.

13. He worked hard. He succeeded. → He worked hard, ___ he succeeded. (so)

→ He worked hard, so he succeeded.

14. She is beautiful. She is intelligent. → She is ___ beautiful ___ intelligent. (not only...but also)

→ She is not only beautiful but also intelligent.

15. He was late. He missed the train. → He missed the train ___ he was late. (because)

→ He missed the train because he was late.

16. It was raining. We went out. → We went out ___ it was raining. (although)

→ We went out although it was raining.

17. She is rich. She is not happy. → ___ she is rich, she is not happy. (although)

→ Although she is rich, she is not happy.

18. He studied hard. He failed. → ___ he studied hard, he failed. (although)

→ Although he studied hard, he failed.

19. I was tired. I went to bed. → I was tired, ___ I went to bed. (so)

→ I was tired, so I went to bed.

20. He is tall. His brother is taller. → His brother is taller ___ him. (than)

→ His brother is taller than him.
